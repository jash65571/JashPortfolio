01 / CONTEXT

Carry one item. Trade with a neighbor. Leave everyone with what they want. The rules fit in a sentence; the order of your choices makes the puzzle.

A puzzle game needs its rules, hints, undo, and saved progress to agree. If the interface has a different idea of a legal move than the solver, the whole game loses trust.

02 / ENGINEERING DECISIONS
01

One source of truth for a move.

Framework-independent rules define legal trades and winning states. Input paths share the same trade command. The React interface displays the state; the game rules do not depend on React.

02

Hints that meet you where you are.

A breadth-first search explores legal states from the current holdings. It finds a path forward after the player takes a different route, instead of replaying a fixed answer. Undo brings back a prior snapshot.

03

Grow content without breaking progress.

The campaign contains 100 frozen level definitions across ten chapters. Content tooling checks solvability and structure. Existing level IDs stay stable, and versioned saves protect the progression contract.

04

Make the phone the starting point.

The browser game uses a shared DOM interface, large touch targets, clear item ownership, keyboard controls, and a service worker for offline use. Automated checks cover rules, content, persistence, and browser flows.
