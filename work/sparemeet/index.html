01 / CONTEXT

Two phone screens can share a model number and still be very different products. Condition, compatibility, testing, and stock history need to survive every step of the sale.

A repair shop needs to manage individual used parts, explain their condition, and keep a public catalog aligned with staff inventory. A simple product table cannot capture that whole lifecycle.

02 / ENGINEERING DECISIONS
01

Model the part before the storefront.

The application separates inventory state from publication state. A part can move through review, availability, reservation, sale, return, repair, or archive. A shared lifecycle module checks allowed transitions and records the right kind of history event.

02

Treat checkout as a request.

Checkout revalidates the cart against the live catalog before creating a pending-review order. A repeat submission uses an idempotency key. Stock reservation belongs to shop confirmation, so sending a request and accepting an order remain separate actions.

03

Build for the people at the counter.

The staff flow covers intake, condition, compatibility, nested physical locations, commercial details, and review. Location records connect each part to its shop, rack, and bin. The public side turns that inventory into a searchable catalog. Role checks protect staff and financial actions.

04

Plan for running it.

The repo includes migration and backup tools, a restore test, deployment smoke checks, accessibility checks, and staff guides. Production infrastructure, real inventory onboarding, and shop policy decisions remain part of the launch work.
